When the user brain dumps, the features or exclusions they name are examples off the top of their head, not requirements or rules. Weigh them as signals; research and reasoning decide.

They also can't dump everything up front: a question asked in context (in a page or in chat, with options) is what surfaces what they think, sometimes about something else entirely. Ask where a call is taste or a reaction would change the plan. Keep process to yourself; they don't want the "how" explained, only that you track it.

**Why:** 2026-10-30, on B-164: "when I say payroll, and time off … I'm just listing examples … so that you know not to like interpret that as like rules", and "your questions can derive information that I can think of at a certain moment".

**How to apply:** record their examples as "user's examples", never as scope rules; put real questions in the pages and check-ins instead of asking for intent up front. Related: [[scope-with-depth-ladders]], [[decide-surface-architecture-yourself]].
