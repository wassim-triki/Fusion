When the user brain-dumps about a screen (a title, a badge, an icon, a loading shape), don't turn each remark into its own fix. Read them together as evidence that the surface needs rethinking as a whole: one board item per surface, the remarks listed under it as notes, the redesign deciding what changes. Keep separate items only for things that cross surfaces (a product-wide rule like no em dashes) or real bugs (kept as "prove gone after the redesign").

**Why:** the user, 2026-11-09, during the B-331 walkthrough after I had boarded ~30 one-line fixes: "Do not interpret my brain dumps as individual tasks just to fix what you might think is bad … my brain dump could be interpreted as a general overhaul to the surface, not individual fixes. So the tasks shouldn't come out of the individual things I say, but the things as a whole."

Also keep functional defects apart from visual verdicts. User, same walk, after "Performance needs a visual rework" had absorbed a broken praise flow: "there's a difference between saying Performance will need a visual rework and actual core functionalities that are misused, not implemented, or buggy. The former should be decoupled from the latter." A broken or missing function gets its own item (or a "Bug:" line) even when the surface is slated for redesign.

**How to apply:** while capturing notes, file them under the surface's one item. When building, design the surface fresh (canvas first if it's taste, [[canvas-first-for-taste-rework]]) rather than patching each remark. Related: [[brain-dumps-are-examples]], [[walkthrough-one-step-at-a-time]].
